Prototype, running on a JAM testnet

An exchange with no vault.

JAMX matches orders on JAM and settles bitcoin on Bitcoin. The seller pays the buyer directly and proves it, and the service releases the money when the proof checks out. No key anywhere in it can move more than one person's funds.

Why this exists

Every exchange hack is the same hack.

An exchange keeps its customers' coins in wallets it controls. Whoever gets the keys gets the coins. On 29 October 2026 about $6.3 billion left Binance's hot wallets in one night. Bitcoin itself was never touched. The vault was. Binance covered the loss from its own funds; FTX, in 2022, could not.

Most on-chain alternatives rebuilt the vault in another shape: a bridge behind a committee of signers, an administrator key, a solver with a database. Seven of them failed on security in 2025 and 2026.

$6.3Btaken from one exchange's hot wallets on 29 October 2026
290,000 BTCsit with custodians on other chains. Signer networks hold about 4,400.
5 of 7security failures of on-chain venues broke at a key that held pooled funds, or at an operator's server

JAMX removes the part that breaks. It never holds the bitcoin, and no key can move anyone else's tokens.

How it works

How a trade settles

  1. You sign an order

    It is a message, not a transaction. It stays off-chain until it trades, so posting a quote costs nothing.

    Off-chain

  2. Each round has one price

    Every six seconds anyone can bundle the open orders into a round. All of them trade at a single clearing price, so there is no place in line to race for.

    On JAM

  3. The seller pays you on Bitcoin

    Your payment is locked in the service. The seller sends the bitcoin to your own address on Bitcoin's chain. It never enters JAM and is never wrapped.

    On Bitcoin

  4. The proof releases the money

    The service checks the payment against Bitcoin's block headers, which it follows itself, and pays the seller. If no proof arrives in time, by Bitcoin's own clock, you are refunded and receive the seller's bond.

    On JAM

Your keys, with a way back

Holding your own keys should not mean one mistake costs everything. Each account sets its own rules, and weakening them always waits out a delay.

  • A second devicethat can trade but cannot withdraw.
  • A guardianwho can freeze the account and cannot spend from it.
  • Recovery by people you chooseif you lose every device.
  • A daily limitso a stolen key, or a forced signature, moves only so much.

    Why JAM
    
    
    Why this needs JAM
    
    
    Checking a Bitcoin payment means hashing headers and walking Merkle paths. Checking a round of orders means hundreds of signatures. On most chains that work is too expensive to do on-chain, so projects hand it to an operator and ask you to trust the operator. JAM splits a service in two.
    
    
    
      
    
        Refine
        
    Heavy work, re-run by auditors
    
        
    Signatures, proof-of-work and Merkle proofs are checked here, off-chain and in parallel across cores. Validators chosen at random run it again, so nobody has to trust whoever ran it first.
    
        
    
          
    budget per work-package
    5,000,000,000 gas
    
          
    one signature
    3,000,000 gas
    
          
    one Bitcoin receipt
    100,000 gas
    
        
    
      
    
      
    
        Accumulate
        
    Small, on-chain, final
    
        
    Funds are checked against state, the round gets its single price, and balances move, all or nothing. This is the scarce part, and where the design spends its effort.

    What JAM does not change
    
        
      
          
    • Speed. Rounds every six seconds, settled in twelve to eighteen. This is not a venue for trades decided in milliseconds.
      • 
          
    • Liquidity. A new venue starts with none. A market maker has to bring it.
      • 
          
    • Money from banks. A real or a dollar is a bank's liability on any chain. That side needs a licensed issuer.
      • 
          
    • Bugs. Removing keys moves the risk into the service's own code. The audit comes before anything else.

    Who it is for
    
    
    Built for whoever would otherwise hold the key
    
    
    Exchanges have repaid their customers after each recent theft, so safety alone does not move most people. It matters most to the institution that would have to do the repaying.
    
    
    
      
    
        Pilot
        
    Banks and token issuers
    
        
    Offer bitcoin trading without holding your customers' coins. You issue the token, keep the allowlist and can freeze your own liability. Nobody can drain you of what you never held.
    
      
    
      
    
        Pilot
        
    People who want bitcoin in their own wallet
    
        
    Buy from your bank's app, receive on your own address, and keep a way back if you lose the phone.
    
      
    
      
    
        Pilot
        
    Market makers
    
        
    One price per round and no latency race. Orders pegged to a reference price and cancels that land before the round are on the roadmap, because nobody should quote into six seconds without them.
    
      
    
      
    
        After the pilot
        
    Companies and trading desks
    
        
    About a quarter of the crypto value declared in Brazil moves outside exchanges, between companies, with one side sending first. Delivery against payment removes that risk.

    What is settled and what is not
    
        
    The mechanism works and is measured. The open questions are legal and commercial: whether a venue with no operator counts as a service provider in Brazil, who issues the token, and who makes the market. The roadmap puts those first because code cannot answer them.

    Questions
    
    
    Plain answers
    
    
    
      
    Is it live?
    No. It is a prototype on a local JAM testnet with test money. JAM's main network has not launched, and the service has not had an independent audit.
    
      
    Does JAMX ever hold my bitcoin?
    No. Bitcoin moves from the seller's wallet to yours on Bitcoin's own chain. The service reads proof that it happened and nothing more.
    
      
    What does it hold, then?
    Balances of tokens issued by banks, and the payments and bonds locked in open trades. No key can move them. Only the rules in the code can, which is why the code is the thing to audit.
    
      
    What if the seller never pays?
    The trade times out by Bitcoin's own clock, not by the service's. You get your money back, plus the seller's bond.
    
      
    Can anyone freeze my funds?
    An issuer can freeze balances of its own token, and every freeze is recorded on-chain. Nobody can freeze or move your bitcoin, because the service never has it.
    
      
    How fast is it?
    A round clears every six seconds and settles on JAM in twelve to eighteen. A payment on Bitcoin then needs confirmations, usually half an hour to an hour. A Lightning option for small amounts is on the roadmap.
    
      
    Is there a token?
    No. Fees are paid in the asset being traded.
    
      
    Where does the idea come from?
    From the essay Bitcoin Wasn't Hacked. Binance Was. JAMX is a working prototype of the design it describes.
